A Field Guide to Distributions

The series gave a page each to a dozen distributions. Out in the wild there are many more, and almost all of them come from a handful of origin stories: counting, waiting, adding, multiplying, dividing, staying in bounds, going round in circles. This guide sorts them by how they arise, so you can recognize one when you meet it.

The family map

Each box is an origin story. Solid chips have their own page in the series; outlined chips have a card further down. Lines join distributions that turn into one another: a sum, a limit, a ratio, a special case. Hover over or focus a chip to pick out its relatives.

01

Flip and count

Repeat a yes/no experiment and count. Change how the trials are drawn, what is counted or how the counts are combined, and a whole family appears.

Own pages: BernoulliBinomialGeometricNegative binomial

02

Wait for it

How long until something happens? A constant rate gives the exponential; rates that change with age, mixtures of rates and random walks that must reach a line give the rest.

Own pages: ExponentialPoissonGamma

03

Add it up

Add many independent pieces, none of them dominant, and the total becomes a bell curve: the central limit theorem. This one has a page of its own.

Own page: Normal

04

Square it

Square normal numbers and add them, and you get squared distances, sums of squared errors and sample variances.

Own page: Chi-square

05

Multiply instead of add

When effects compound, each one a percentage of what is already there, the logarithm turns the product into a sum, and the sum into a bell. The result leans right and never goes below zero.

06

Divide

Divide one noisy number by another that can come close to zero, and now and then the result is enormous. Ratios can be wilder than either of their parts.

07

Stay in bounds

Proportions, probabilities and anything else with a hard floor and ceiling need distributions that live on an interval.

Own page: Beta

08

Go around in circles

Directions, times of day and dates in the year wrap around: 359° is next to 1°, and 23:59 is next to midnight. They need distributions on a circle.

09

Take the biggest

The largest (or smallest) of many values has its own universal shapes: Gumbel, Fréchet and Weibull.

Own page: Extreme values

10

Rich get richer

When size breeds size, there is no typical value: power laws, Pareto and Zipf.

Own page: Power laws

11

Never settle

Sums of heavy-tailed pieces never become a bell, and differences of waits and maxima give bells with fatter, straighter tails. These are the normal’s wilder cousins.

12

Random matrices

Fill a big matrix with noise and look at its eigenvalues. They don’t scatter like independent numbers; they repel each other and settle into shapes of their own, which turn up in physics, statistics and data science.

13

Which distribution?

Match each histogram or scenario to its most likely distribution. Some answers have their own page; others are cards above.
